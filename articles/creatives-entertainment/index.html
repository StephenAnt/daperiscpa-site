Creatives & Entertainment

Royalties, loan-out companies, and planning for income that never arrives on a schedule.

If you act, write, make music, or produce video, your income probably shows up in pieces: a W-2 from one production, a 1099 from a brand deal, a royalty statement months after the work is done. Each piece gets taxed a little differently, and the forms don't always tell you how.

These articles cover two questions NYC creatives ask me most: where royalty income belongs on your return, and whether a loan-out company is worth the extra city tax. (Neither one has a one-size answer, which is why each gets its own article.) For help with your own numbers, here's how we work with creatives and entertainment professionals.
