How Much Does It Cost to Form an LLC in New York?

If you're budgeting for a New York LLC, here's the honest number: $1,000 to $2,000 if you're forming in New York City, and $400 to $800 if you're upstate. That's a wide range, and you're probably wondering why. It comes down to one requirement almost nobody sees coming until it lands in their inbox: publication.

Published September 2nd, 2026 | By Stephen Daperis, CPA

Let's Start with the Easy Part

Filing your Articles of Organization with the New York Department of State costs $200, flat, no matter where in the state you're forming. That's the same for every LLC, and it's about as simple as this process gets. You can now file your Articles of Organization online via the NY Business Express portal, which is the recommended and much faster method, with approval often happening the same day. If you prefer the traditional route, mail-in options to the Division of Corporations in Albany still exist, though they typically take a few weeks to process.

Here's the Part That Actually Costs You Money

Within 120 days of forming your LLC, New York requires you to publish a notice of formation in two newspapers designated by the county clerk in the county where your LLC is registered, one daily paper and one weekly, running for six consecutive weeks. Once that's done, you file a Certificate of Publication with the state, which carries its own $50 fee.

This single requirement is why New York is widely considered the most expensive state to form an LLC in, and it's also the one nobody budgets for, because it doesn't show up until after your LLC already exists.

Why does the cost swing so much? Because the newspapers aren't chosen by the state, they're designated by the county clerk, and each paper sets its own rate. Form in Manhattan and you're likely looking at $1,200 to $2,000 or more. Brooklyn and the other outer boroughs tend to run lower, but it's still a real number. Upstate counties can be just a few hundred dollars. If you have any flexibility on which county your LLC's address lands in, it's worth a phone call to compare before you file, not after.

So What's a Realistic Total?

Put it all together: $200 for the state filing, $50 for the Certificate of Publication, and somewhere between $300 and $2,000 for the newspapers themselves depending on your county. For most people forming in or around New York City, that lands you around $1,000 to $1,750 all in. Upstate, you're often looking at $550 to $900.

What Happens If You Just... Don't Publish?

You can't skip it, unfortunately, though people try. An LLC that misses the 120-day window doesn't get automatically dissolved, but it does lose its authority to conduct business in New York until it files the Certificate of Publication, and it can't maintain a lawsuit in New York courts in the meantime. Catching up later won't cost more in state fees, but you're sitting exposed for however long you wait, and that's not where you want to be if a dispute happens to come up during that window.

What About After Formation?

Once publication is behind you, the ongoing costs settle down. You'll file a Biennial Statement every two years for $9, due in the same calendar month your Articles of Organization were originally filed. If your LLC has New York-source income or deductions, you'll also owe an annual filing fee tiered by income, generally starting around $25 and scaling up from there.

One More Thing to Keep in Mind

None of the numbers above include a registered agent service if you'd rather not be your own, an operating agreement if you want one drafted for you, or your EIN, which is free directly from the IRS no matter what anyone tries to sell you. And if you're opening a restaurant, salon, or bar, remember the LLC is just one piece. Industry-specific licensing and permits sit on top of all of this, and they have nothing to do with the entity itself.

Keep It Simple / Key Takeaway 🍕

Think of forming a New York LLC as two separate bills, not one. The state fee is the same $200 no matter what. The publication requirement is where the real money moves, and it's almost entirely a function of which county your LLC calls home. Know that number before you file, not after the newspaper invoice shows up in the mail.
